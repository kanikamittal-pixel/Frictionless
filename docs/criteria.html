[01] What Outpost doesOverview

Every application, decided — with a reason.

The expansion team picks the brand's industry and business model and uploads the applications and territories sheets. For every partner Outpost returns Approve, Conditional, Hold or Reject, the reason, the conditions to close, who they competed with, a simulated “path to yes”, and a dated onboarding plan in the partner's language. The team can then ask why, or give new information and see exactly who moves before anything is saved.

Design principles

The engine decides; the AI never scoresDecisions come from deterministic code — the same file always gives the same answer. The AI reads messy text, explains, translates and turns chat into edits.
Data problems → Hold, not RejectA blank amount or a city/pin conflict costs the partner one question, not their application.
One store per pin, alwaysExisting stores included. Usable slots are capped by pins that are still free.
Best partner wins the slotA strong runner-up for a pin is re-scored on a free pin in the same territory instead of being thrown away.
The team decidesOverrides need a reason; rubric changes are previewed and approved as new versions; every change is logged and undoable.
Show raw values, not just scores“₹18L vs Tier 3 minimum ₹20L (0.90×) · batch median ₹30L” — every number is traceable.

Decision vocabulary

ApproveWins a slot and scores ≥ the tier bar (T1 70 · T2 66 · T3 62), nothing left to close.
ConditionalWins a slot (score ≥ 55) but has conditions — no space yet, funding gap, relocation. Slot held 60 days.
On holdScore 45–55, waitlisted behind better partners, outside the plan, or missing data.
RejectScore under 45, or failed a knockout above.

[05] Decision pipeline — step by stepFig. 02

Fig. 02 · The order the engine runs in. Grey steps are deterministic code; the blue step uses AI with a rule-based fallback. Outcomes on the right end the path for that application.

Phase 1 territory gate

Scenario A — strict. If at least one application in the batch is in a preferred territory, only in-territory applications go on to slots; the others are held on the waitlist as “outside the expansion area” (they still get a score so the team can see latent demand).
Scenario B — fallback. If none are, nobody is held back: every application continues with the flag “Not a preferred location for company”, a strategic-fit score of 2/10, and a condition that the team must open a territory.

Knockouts (in this order)

  1. Data checks — Blank amount, city/pin mismatch, notes that contradict the form → Hold for one answer.
  2. Pin already has a store — Never two stores in one pin → Reject, with nearby open pins suggested.
  3. Pin outside every territory — → Hold on the waitlist (if any application in the batch is inside the plan).
  4. No usable slot — Territory has 0 open slots, or every pin is already served → Reject.
  5. Capital too low — Under 50% of the tier setup cost → Reject.
[06] Scoring rubricRubric v1 · 100 points

Five dimensions, thirteen metrics.

Each metric is scored 0–10. Partner fit (7 metrics) and location fit (6 metrics) are each weighted to 100 and blended 50/50. The result is shown as five dimensions so the reason is easy to read.

[08] AllocationFig. 03

One store per pin; the best partners win the slots.

  1. Usable slots per territory = min(open slots, pins without an existing store). A pin listed in two territories counts for the higher-priority one.
  2. Pin contest. Among eligible applications on the same pin, the best score keeps it.
  3. Relocation. Each runner-up is re-scored on every free pin of its territory (preferring pins no one else asked for). If its best re-score still clears 55, it joins the slot race with a “relocate” condition.
  4. Slot race. Pin holders and relocated runners-up are ranked by score; tie-break capital → site readiness → sooner launch → lower ID. The top N take the N usable slots; the rest are waitlisted with their rank, and the first is named “next in line” on the slot holders.
  5. Proximity check. Two new stores closer than 2 km get a catchment-overlap flag.

Plan timing rules

Offer letter day 5 · KYC day 10 · site audit day 14 (or site found by day 60 if no space) · agreement 7 days later · fit-out 5 weeks (≤600 sq ft), 7 (≤1,200) or 9 weeks · licences agreement + 21 days · hire 4 staff 3 weeks before handover · owner training 13 days at the nearest store · soft launch 2 days after fit-out · grand opening on the target date if feasible, otherwise a week after soft launch · 30-day review with a “ready to run alone” sign-off · 90-day review. A festival deadline (e.g. Diwali, 8 Nov 2026) is checked against the plan.

[10] Feedback and improve loopsFig. 04

Ask why. Say what changed. See who moves.

Fig. 04 · The chat loop (top) and the improve loop (bottom). Orange boxes are decision gates: nothing changes until the team confirms.

  • Event-sourced data. The uploaded file is never modified; every change is an edit on top of it, so any decision can be traced to the exact facts that produced it, and any change can be undone.
  • Whole-batch re-runs. One update can change rivals' decisions (e.g. a withdrawal frees a slot and the next in line moves up); the preview shows those knock-on changes before Apply.
  • Upgrades from Reject are tagged “team to confirm”.
  • Rubric versions. The pattern finder proposes changes of at most 5 points per weight, says when evidence is thin (< 5 recorded outcomes), and keeps each group at 100. The team previews the impact on the current batch and approves version N+1.

[13] Assumptions and limitsRead before deciding

What the numbers rest on.

  • Tier minimums and space ranges are benchmarks (eyewear: Lenskart's published ~₹35L regular / ~₹20L “Lite” formats, 250–600 sq ft), not given in the case. The team can tune them before upload.
  • Score cut-offs (tier bars 70/66/62, review band 45–55) are starting values to be tuned against which partners actually open and pay back.
  • No store performance data. Sheet 3 has no sales or payback, so existing stores are assumed healthy; payback is a category benchmark.
  • No geography below pin code. About 60 well-known pins are on a built-in locality map; others use an AI estimate or the city baseline. Distances are approximate.
  • Competition means cannibalisation of the brand's own stores and white space; no competitor data is uploaded.
  • Preferred language counts in “local nuance” (10% of partner fit), following the team's applicant rubric.
  • Financial strength beyond stated investment and traits like coachability are checked in the interview, not scored.
